# Microsoft Hackathon 2026

### Challenge: Collaboration using GitHub Planning & Tracking Tools in the Agentic Age

Welcome! 👋 This repository is the home base for **one of the three hackathon challenges**. It's where
ideas for this challenge get submitted, where the project board lives, and where teams hand in their
final projects.

**➡️ [Submit an idea](../../issues/new?template=1-challenge-idea.yml) · [See the board](https://github.com/users/reneexeener/projects/59) · [Browse all ideas](../../issues?q=is%3Aissue+label%3A%22type%3A+idea%22)**

---

## The challenge in one paragraph

Software teams have spent years building rituals around planning and tracking work — issues, boards,
standups, estimates, retros. Then coding agents arrived and started doing some of the work. So: **what
does good collaboration look like when part of your team isn't human?** Build something that answers
that, using GitHub's planning and tracking tools (Issues, Projects, milestones, sub-issues, Actions,
the GraphQL API) together with AI agents.

That's deliberately broad. A dashboard, a bot, a workflow, a Copilot extension, a genuinely better
board layout — all fair game.

### Some themes to get you started

These are **prompts, not categories.** They exist because a blank page is hard, not because your idea
has to fit one of them. If yours sits between two of them, or somewhere else entirely, that's a good
sign — pick the closest when you submit (or skip it) and describe what you actually mean in the issue.

| Theme | Things it might look like |
| --- | --- |
| 🗂️ **Planning & tracking** | Issues, Projects, milestones, roadmaps, estimation |
| 🤖 **Agentic workflows** | Copilot coding agent, AI-assisted planning, triage, review |
| ⚙️ **Automation** | Actions, webhooks, bots, the GraphQL API |
| 📊 **Insights & metrics** | Dashboards, reporting, velocity, project analytics |
| 📚 **Onboarding & docs** | Getting new contributors productive fast |
| ♿ **Accessibility & inclusion** | Collaboration that works for everyone |

The theme is only used for grouping and browsing the board. Nothing is judged on it, and nobody will
tell you your idea is in the wrong one.

## What you need to do

### 1. Submit at least one idea 💡 *(everyone, before the hackathon starts)*

[**Open an idea issue →**](../../issues/new?template=1-challenge-idea.yml)

Even if you already know what you're building, submit it. Two reasons:

1. It gets you hands-on with Issues and Projects, which is half the point of this challenge.
2. Every idea goes into a shared pool. Ideas nobody has time to build are up for grabs — and we keep
   the pool for next year.

Rough ideas are welcome. You'll be asked what problem it solves, roughly which theme it's closest to,
and where an agent does real work. "Not sure yet" is a valid answer to that last one — organizers will
help shape it.

### 2. Build it 🔨 *(during the hackathon)*

Your **code lives wherever you want** — a separate repo is completely fine and usually easier.

You're *encouraged* to track your team's work here as [team task issues](../../issues/new?template=3-team-task.yml)
so you're actually practising the thing the challenge is about. **This is optional**, and there's a
tradeoff worth understanding: everything in this repo is visible to every other participant. Some
teams like that — you can see what others are trying and borrow ideas. Some teams would rather work
quietly. Both are fine. If you'd rather keep your day-to-day work private, use your own repo's board
and only submit the idea and final submission issues here.

### 3. Submit your project 🏁 *(by the deadline)*

[**Open a submission issue →**](../../issues/new?template=2-project-submission.yml)

One per team. Submit before the deadline even if you're still polishing — you can edit the issue right
up until judging.

## The project board

Everything lands on the **[Ideas & Submissions board](https://github.com/users/reneexeener/projects/59)**.

Items move through these statuses:

| Status | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| 💡 Idea submitted | Just proposed, waiting on organizer triage |
| ✅ Triaged | Reviewed — open for any team to adopt |
| 🙋 Claimed by team | A team has picked it up |
| 🚧 In progress | Actively being built |
| 📦 Submitted | Handed in for judging |
| 🏆 Judged | Judges have reviewed it |

Alongside Status, each item has **Submission type**, **Theme**, **Team**, **Effort**, and — for finished
projects — **Code repo** and **Demo link**. Try grouping the board by Theme or Team to see how the same
items tell different stories.

### Adopting someone else's idea

Ideas labelled [`status: looking-for-team`](../../issues?q=is%3Aissue+label%3A%22status%3A+looking-for-team%22)
are unclaimed. To take one: comment on the issue saying your team is claiming it, and an organizer will
assign you and flip it to `status: claimed`. Credit the original author in your submission.

## How projects are judged

| Criterion | What we're looking for |
| --- | --- |
| **Answers the challenge** | Does it actually say something about collaboration in the agentic age? |
| **Agentic depth** | Are agents doing meaningful work, with sensible human checkpoints — or is AI bolted on? |
| **Craft** | Does it work? Can someone else run it from your README? |
| **Collaboration, shown** | Your issues and board are part of the submission. We'd rather see a messy honest trail than a tidy fake one. |
| **Demo** | Can you make a stranger understand it in two minutes? |

Being unfinished is not disqualifying. A half-built thing with clear thinking beats a polished thing
with none.

## Ground rules

- **Ideas are public to all participants.** Don't submit anything you're not comfortable sharing —
  and don't put confidential material, credentials, or personal data in issues.
- **Be generous.** If someone adopts your idea, that's a compliment. Credit each other.
- **Search before you submit.** If your idea is close to an existing one, link it instead of duplicating —
  or team up.
- **Ask early.** Stuck on whether something fits the challenge? Ask before you build for a day.
